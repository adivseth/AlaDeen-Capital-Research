## 1. Investment vs. Speculation

**Graham's Definition (Security Analysis, Ch. 4):**
> *Paraphrase:* an investment is an operation that, after thorough analysis, offers safety of principal and an adequate return; anything that does not meet that test is speculation.

Three elements of investment:
1. **Thorough analysis** — qualitative and quantitative research
2. **Safety of principal** — not permanent loss of capital
3. **Adequate return** — not maximum return; *adequate* and sustainable

**Implications for analysis:**
- Any stock bought without thorough analysis = speculation
- Buying at any price without margin of safety = speculation
- Day trading, momentum trading = speculation by definition

## 2. Mr. Market & Margin of Safety

### Mr. Market (Intelligent Investor, Ch. 8)
Graham's allegory: The market is a business partner who quotes you a buy or sell price every day. His prices fluctuate with his mood (fear/greed), not with the underlying value of the business.

**The intelligent investor:**
- Buys when Mr. Market is **fearful** (price << value)
- Sells when Mr. Market is **euphoric** (price >> value)
- Ignores Mr. Market entirely when prices are fair
- Never sells just because prices fall; never buys just because prices rise

### Margin of Safety (Intelligent Investor, Ch. 20)
> *Paraphrase:* the margin of safety is the gap between the earnings yield you buy at and the bond yield; that cushion absorbs analytical errors and bad luck.

**Practical application:**

```
Margin of Safety (MoS) = (Intrinsic Value – Market Price) / Intrinsic Value × 100%

Graham's rule: Never buy unless MoS ≥ 33% (1/3 discount to intrinsic value)
For more uncertain businesses: MoS ≥ 50%
```

**Why MoS matters:**
- Valuation models contain errors in input assumptions
- Future business conditions are uncertain
- MoS provides a cushion against being wrong

**MoS by business type:**
| Type | Minimum MoS |
|------|------------|
| High-quality franchise, predictable cash flows | 25–33% |
| Cyclical business | 40–50% |
| Small/mid-cap with limited track record | 50% |
| Deep value / turnaround | 50%+ |

## 3. Defensive Investor Criteria

**Source: The Intelligent Investor, Ch. 14**

For the **defensive (passive) investor** who wants safety, simplicity, and adequate return:

### Minimum Criteria (adapt to Indian market)

| Criterion | Graham Original (US) | India Adaptation |
|-----------|---------------------|-----------------|
| **Size** | Revenue > $100M | Revenue > ₹500 Cr or Market Cap > ₹1,000 Cr |
| **Financial condition** | Current Ratio ≥ 2.0; LT Debt ≤ Net Current Assets | Same |
| **Earnings stability** | Positive EPS every year for 10 years | Positive PAT every year for 7+ years |
| **Dividend record** | Uninterrupted dividends for 20 years | 10 years of dividends (India market younger) |
| **Earnings growth** | +33% EPS growth over 10 years (3-yr avg vs 3-yr avg) | Same |
| **P/E limit** | ≤ 15× (3-yr avg EPS) | ≤ 20× (India higher growth justifies premium) |
| **P/B limit** | P/B × P/E ≤ 22.5 | Same — P/B × P/E ≤ 22.5 |

### Graham's Combined Multiplier Rule
```
P/B × P/E ≤ 22.5

Example: If P/E = 15, then P/B must be ≤ 1.5
         If P/E = 9, then P/B must be ≤ 2.5
```

### Portfolio Construction (Defensive)
- **50/50 bonds-equities** as baseline; rebalance when stocks exceed 75% or fall below 25%
- **Diversification**: 10–30 stocks; no single holding > 5% of portfolio
- **Avoid**: "Growth stocks" where high price embeds optimistic expectations that must be met to earn even adequate returns

## 4. Enterprising Investor Criteria

**Source: The Intelligent Investor, Ch. 15**

For the **enterprising (active) investor** willing to put in more work for higher returns:

### Bargain Issues (Quantitative Filters)

**Net Current Asset Value Stocks:**
- Stock price < 2/3 × NCAV (see Section 6)
- Company must have positive earnings (profitable; not just asset play)

**Low-Multiple Stocks:**
- P/E < 9 (half of Dow average in Graham's era) — adapt to < 12× in India
- P/B < 1.2

**Special Situations:**
- Mergers and acquisitions in progress
- Liquidations
- Rights offerings below market price

### Secondary Companies (Relatively Neglected Stocks)
- Good financial condition (but not blue-chip)
- P/E < 75% of average large-cap P/E
- Recent losses or dividend cuts = opportunity if financials remain sound
- Net working capital covers a substantial portion of market cap

### Stocks to Avoid (Graham's Exclusions)
- High-profile "growth" stocks with P/E > 20× already embedding high growth
- Concept/story stocks with no earnings history
- New issues (IPOs) — Graham is deeply skeptical
- Companies with declining book value per share over 10 years

## 5. Intrinsic Value Approaches

### Graham's Valuation Formula (The Intelligent Investor)
```
V* = EPS × (8.5 + 2g) × (4.4 / Y)

Where:
  V*   = Estimated intrinsic value
  EPS  = Normalised trailing 12-month earnings per share
  8.5  = P/E ratio for a no-growth company (Graham's assumption)
  g    = Expected annual growth rate over next 7–10 years (%)
  4.4  = Graham's "ideal" AAA bond yield (baseline)
  Y    = Current AAA-rated bond yield (or 10-yr G-Sec yield for India)
```

**Example (Indian stock):**
```
EPS = ₹50, g = 12%, 10-yr G-Sec yield = 7.1%
V* = 50 × (8.5 + 24) × (4.4/7.1) = 50 × 32.5 × 0.62 = ₹1,007

If CMP = ₹700 → MoS = 30.5% → Interesting entry point
If CMP = ₹950 → MoS = 5.7%  → Too close to intrinsic; wait
```

**Limitations of formula:**
- Sensitive to growth rate assumption
- Does not account for debt or capital intensity
- Best for simple, stable businesses with long earnings history
- Use as one input; not standalone

### Earning Power Value (Security Analysis)
```
Earning Power Value = Normalised EBIT × (1 – t) / WACC
                   = Normalised NOPAT / WACC

Where:
  Normalised EBIT = Average of past 5–7 years' operating profit
                    (strip cyclical peaks and troughs)
```

**Graham & Dodd's view**: A company worth more than its earning power value is priced on hope. A company worth less may be a bargain.

## 6. Graham's Net Current Asset Value (NCAV)

**Source: Security Analysis, Ch. 43**

### NCAV Formula
```
NCAV = Current Assets – Total Liabilities (including preferred stock at liquidation value)

Per share NCAV = NCAV / Shares Outstanding

Conservative adjustment (Graham's preferred):
  Cash & equivalents:     100% of book value
  Receivables:            80% of book value (for collectability discount)
  Inventory:              67% of book value
  Other current assets:   50% of book value
  Fixed assets:           15% of book value (liquidation value)

Net Liquidation Value = Adjusted Assets – Total Liabilities
```

### NCAV Investing Rules
1. Buy at **< 2/3 of NCAV** (stock at deep discount to net current assets)
2. Company must still be **profitable** (not a melting ice cube)
3. Management must not be **destroying remaining value** (check cash burn)
4. **Diversify** — hold 20–30 NCAV stocks; individual outcomes are unpredictable

### NCAV in India (modern adaptation)
- Pure NCAV stocks are rare in a bull market
- More useful: compare NCAV to market cap to find asset support
- Even if NCAV < market cap, a stock trading at < 1× Book Value with ROE > 12% may offer value

## 7. Security Analysis: Earnings Quality

**Source: Security Analysis, Chapters 31–38**

### Red Flags in Income Statement

**Non-Recurring Items (Chapter 32)**
- Exceptional gains/losses must be stripped from normalised earnings
- Watch: Gains on asset sales presented as operating income
- Watch: "Other income" comprising gains on treasury operations (India-specific)
- Rule: If non-recurring items have recurred 3+ years → they are recurring; include

**Subsidiary/Associate Earnings (Chapter 33)**
- Income from associates at higher-than-proportional share = manipulation signal
- Check: Is dividend income from subsidiaries matching reported profits?
- Watch: Profits reported from unlisted subsidiaries that are not cash-accretive

**Depreciation Manipulation (Chapter 34)**
- Unusually low depreciation rate → inflated profits
- Compare: Gross block age vs. depreciation rate
- Acid test: Capex / Depreciation ratio
  - < 1.0 over 5 years → ageing assets, under-investment
  - > 3.0 → aggressive expansion; check FCFF

**Adjusting EPS for Quality:**
```
Adjusted EPS = (PAT – Exceptional gains + Exceptional losses) / Shares
             = Normalised for one-off items

Normalised EPS = Average Adjusted EPS over 5 years
               (use in Graham formula and defensive screen)
```

### Accruals Quality
```
Accruals Ratio = (Net Income – OCF) / Average Total Assets

High positive accruals (> 10%) = earnings not backed by cash → red flag
Negative accruals                 = cash earnings exceed reported → positive signal
```

## 8. Security Analysis: Balance Sheet Analysis

**Source: Security Analysis, Chapters 42–45**

### Book Value Analysis (Chapter 42)
```
Book Value per Share = (Total Equity – Goodwill – Intangibles – DTA) / Shares

Tangible Book Value (TBV) = Book Value excluding intangibles and goodwill
```

- For manufacturing/asset-heavy companies: Compare P/TBV
- Goodwill should be scrutinised — paid for acquisitions; impairment risk
- DTA (Deferred Tax Asset): Valid only if profitable earnings stream assured

### Current Asset Quality (Chapter 43)
```
Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities  (Graham minimum: 2.0)
Quick Ratio  = (CA – Inventory) / CL                 (Graham minimum: 1.0)
Cash Ratio   = Cash / Current Liabilities             (Stress test)
```

**Working Capital Analysis:**
```
Net Working Capital = Current Assets – Current Liabilities
NWC per share vs. stock price: If NWC/share > 50% of stock price → asset support
```

**Receivable Days (DSO):**
```
DSO = (Debtors / Revenue) × 365

Rising DSO without revenue growth = Aggressive recognition or collection risk
Compare to sector average; flag if > 30% above peers
```

**Inventory Turns:**
```
Inventory Turns = Revenue / Average Inventory

Falling turns = Slow-moving stock; possible write-off risk
```

### Long-Term Debt Analysis
```
Debt/Equity Ratio        = Total Debt / Equity      (Graham: < 1.0 for defensive)
Interest Coverage Ratio  = EBIT / Interest Expense   (Graham: ≥ 5× preferred)
Debt/EBITDA              = Total Debt / EBITDA       (≤ 3× comfortable; ≤ 2× preferred)
```

### Capital Structure Quality Flags (Security Analysis, Ch. 40)
- **Complex capital structure** (multiple classes of equity, convertible bonds, ESOP overhang) → apply 10–20% discount to fair value
- **Off-balance sheet guarantees** → add to debt
- **Related party transactions** → red flag; scrutinise for value transfer
- **Promoter pledge** → high pledge % indicates financial stress of promoter

## 9. Capital Structure Analysis

**Source: Security Analysis, Chapters 26, 40**

### Senior Securities with Speculative Features (Ch. 22–26)
- Convertible bonds → option value; check conversion premium
- Preferred stock → check cumulative dividend arrears; coverage
- Warrants outstanding → dilution effect on EPS and book value per share

### Capitalization Structure
```
Total Capital = Equity + Total Debt + Preference Capital
Capital Gearing = Debt / (Debt + Equity)

Low gearing (< 30%) → financial safety
Medium gearing (30–60%) → moderate risk
High gearing (> 60%) → speculative; requires strong and stable earnings
```

### Preferred Stock (India: Preference Shares)
- Check for cumulative dividend arrears
- Redeemable preference shares = quasi-debt; include in debt for leverage analysis
- Graham: Preference shares only attractive if company earnings cover preference dividend by 3× or more

## 10. Dividend Factor

**Source: Security Analysis, Chapters 29–30; Intelligent Investor**

### Graham's Dividend Philosophy
1. Dividends are **proof of earning power** — a company that can pay consistently demonstrates real profits, not accounting profits
2. Retained earnings must earn at least as much as shareholders could earn elsewhere; otherwise, pay out as dividends
3. **Optimal payout**: 40–75% of normalised earnings for a mature company

### Dividend Yield as Value Signal
```
Dividend Yield = DPS / CMP × 100%

Graham's rule: In a bear market, buy when dividend yield > 5% (for quality company)
Earnings yield = EPS / CMP × 100%   (inverse of P/E)

If Earnings Yield > 2× 10-yr G-Sec yield → potentially cheap
```

### Dividend Sustainability Check
```
Payout Ratio     = DPS / EPS           (should be < 80% to be sustainable)
Dividend Cover   = EPS / DPS           (minimum 1.25×; prefer 1.5×)
Cash Payout      = DPS × Shares / OCF  (cash flow must support dividends; < 80% of OCF)
```

### Dividend Growth Signal
- Sustained dividend growth > 10% over 10 years = strong signal of fundamental strength
- Dividend cut = serious red flag; re-evaluate investment thesis immediately
- Special dividends = good but not a sustainable signal
